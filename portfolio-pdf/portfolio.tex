\project{1}
  {DJ Board on an STM32}
  {EE 14 Embedded Systems \textbar{} Jan. 2026 to May 2026}
  {A real-time audio effects board that records sound and plays it back with effects like echo, reverse, pitch and speed, all within the STM32's 64KB of on-chip memory. Playback first came out with a high-pitched hiss and clicks, so I wrote a timer-driven state machine that records and plays every sample at a steady rate, and faded each clip in and out. \textbf{An oscilloscope confirmed that the playback matched the original recording.}\label{p1}}
  {dj-circuit.png}{The board: microphone, speaker and effect buttons}
  {dj-scope.png}{Oscilloscope captures of the effect outputs}

\project{2}
  {FPGA Audio Synthesizer}
  {ES 4 Digital Logic Circuits \textbar{} Sept. 2025 to Dec. 2025}
  {An 8-octave synthesizer, from 16Hz to 8kHz, written in SystemVerilog for a MIDI keyboard. My first design divided on the chip to calculate every note and needed over 250\% of the FPGA's space. Since dividing by 2 is the same as shifting bits, I hand-calculated one octave and used bit shifts for the rest, then mixed the notes with PWM. \textbf{The keyboard plays 8 octaves, with chords of up to 6 clear notes.}\label{p2}}
  {fpga-board.png}{The complete board}
  {fpga-octaves.png}{The switch that selects the octave}

\project{3}
  {CTGAN Design Discovery for RRAM}
  {Tufts Emerging Circuits and Systems Lab \textbar{} Sept. 2025 to April 2026}
  {The published framework I extended used a CVAE, a model that averages its training data and smooths away the extreme designs where the best performance is found. I replaced it with a CTGAN, which searches those extremes more aggressively, used the framework's physics model as a referee, and grew the training data tenfold to 5,000 samples. \textbf{The best design reached 25\% higher endurance and 11\% lower switching energy than the published result}, with slightly slower switching (6.57ns vs 4.07ns).\label{p3}}
  {rram-pareto.png}{Final results with 5,000 training samples}
  {rram-evolution.png}{The same search with 500 samples (left) and 2,500 (right)}

\project{4}
  {UAV Pitch Control and RF Signal Restoration}
  {EE 23 Linear Systems \textbar{} Jan. 2026 to May 2026}
  {A UAV that shakes while collecting RF signals blurs them, so keeping it level comes first. Using Routh-Hurwitz analysis, I proved that P and PI controllers can never stabilize it, then searched 15,625 gain combinations for the best PID settings, reaching \textbf{a 52ms rise time and 2.6\% overshoot}. I then restored a blurred, noisy RF signal with a Wiener filter, \textbf{cutting error by 77\%} compared with a direct inverse.\label{p4}}
  {uav-controllers.png}{P, PI, PD and PID controllers on the same UAV model}
  {uav-wiener.png}{Wiener filter error (left) and the restored RF signal (right)}
